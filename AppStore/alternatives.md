# ASO: the reasoning, the competition, and the alternatives

Numbers in brackets are character counts. The limits are 30 for the name, 30 for
the subtitle and 100 for keywords — and the keyword limit alone is counted in
UTF-8 bytes, so a Czech set carries its byte count as well.

Everything below was read off the store on **2 November 2026**: ratings, prices
and languages from the iTunes Search and Lookup APIs (US and CZ storefronts),
subtitles, in-app purchase lists, privacy labels and age ratings from the
product pages, search suggestions from the App Store's own search hints, and
complaints from the last 500 US reviews of seven apps (3,500 reviews in all).
The Search API's order only approximates the real App Store ranking (which is
personalised and includes Search Ads), so the "#1" figures below are indicative.

## What we are up against

The genre — swipe letters on a wheel to fill a small crossword — is one of the
biggest on the store. Forty-one games of it on the US store hold 10.09 million
ratings between them; the top four hold 46 % of those. The largest:

**40 of the 41** carry a *Data Used to Track You* label, 36 declare advertising
in their age rating, and the median of their most expensive listed purchase is
$19.99. The only one whose label reads *Data Not Collected* is *Crossword Jam+* —
the Apple Arcade edition, behind a subscription. Five things follow.

**We cannot win the head terms.** "word game", "word puzzle", "word games" and
"crossword" are held by apps with six- and seven-figure rating counts.
*word* and *game* are in the name because they have to be, not because they will
rank on their own.

**The long tail is winnable, and the name and subtitle decide it.** Searches run
the same day, with the rating count of the apps at the top:

A 772-rating app tops "crossword offline" and a 5,657-rating one tops "word
games no ads", both on the strength of having the words in the name. That is
the whole strategy here: the name and the subtitle buy the modifiers, and the
ratings come later.

**The modifiers people type are "no ads" and "offline".** Apple's suggestions,
in order of popularity: for *word g…* — `word games`, `word games for free`,
`word games for adults`, `word guess`, `word games no ads`; for *crossword o…* —
`crossword offline` first; for *crossword n…* — `crossword no ads`, `crossword
no wifi`; for *word game o…* — `word game offline` first; for *wordscapes* —
`wordscapes no ads` third; for *word games no…* — `no ads`, `free … no ads`, `no
wifi`, `no internet`. And for *games without…*: `wifi`, `ads`, `internet`,
`in-app purchases`, `service`, `timers`. This game can claim every one of them
honestly, and almost nobody else in the genre can.

**The whole genre is monetised, including the apps whose names promise it is
not.** The only sizeable "no ads" apps are two by one developer, and both sell
coins and a $12.99 "Play Unlimited"; 38 % of *Word Puzzle: No Ads, No Wifi!*'s
1–2★ reviews are about coins and paywalls, and 29 % about having to rate it 5★
to get coins ("Doesn't let you play till you rate the game 5 stars. What a
scam!"). *Zen Word*, *Otium Word* and *Word Relax* sell weekly subscriptions
for "no ads rights". So "no ads" alone is a promise this audience has learnt to
distrust, and the listing says the rest of it too: **nothing to buy**, coins
earned by playing and never sold, no account, no tracking, no network code.

**The calm angle is the genre's own language, and its own failure.** Reviewers
use *relax*, *calm*, *peaceful* or *soothing* constantly (109 of Word Calm's last
500 reviews, 80 of Word Oasis's, 66 of Zen Word's), and the biggest "calm" apps
are the ones with the most ad complaints. So *calm* is in the subtitle and
*relaxing* in the keywords — and the screenshots show it rather than claim it.

## What players complain about, and what they ask for

Share of the 1–2★ reviews in each app's last 500 (keyword matching, approximate):

| Complaint | Wordscapes | Word Cookies | Words of Wonders | Zen Word | Word Calm | Word Oasis | "No Ads, No Wifi" |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Ads | **72 %** | 22 % | 64 % | **70 %** | 43 % | 68 % | 3 % |
| Ads after every level / mid-level | 33 % | 8 % | 28 % | 34 % | 11 % | 16 % | 0 % |
| Coins, prices, paywall | 18 % | 18 % | 23 % | 14 % | 19 % | 21 % | **38 %** |
| Too easy / repetitive | 8 % | 3 % | 6 % | 7 % | 11 % | 10 % | 0 % |
| Valid words rejected / obscure words | 2 % | 1 % | 0 % | 5 % | **10 %** | 5 % | 0 % |
| Pop-ups, offers, bundles | 10 % | 15 % | 4 % | 11 % | 2 % | 2 % | 0 % |
| Forced 5★ rating for coins | 0 % | 0 % | 0 % | 0 % | 0 % | 0 % | **29 %** |

What the answers in the listing are built on:

- **Ads** — the first screenshot, the name, and the first line of the
  description. Reviewers say outright they would pay to be rid of them ("Please
  make a version of this ad free and I will gladly pay for it", Wordscapes); the
  ones who did are angry ("I just paid the 9.99 for no ads, but I'm still getting
  them", Word Oasis). Several play in airplane mode to escape them.
- **Coins** — "Hints you earn, never buy", in a screenshot and a section of the
  description. A no-ads game with coins on its hint buttons will be read as the
  next coin shop unless it says so.
- **Valid words rejected** — "Every real word counts": anything the wheel can
  spell that is a real word is a bonus word.
- **Timers and "just let me play"** — "No rush" and "No countdown, no lives, no
  energy". Wordscapes reviewers complain that its timed mode cannot be turned
  off; Word Cookies reviewers ask to opt out of events.
- **Definitions** — wished for in Zen Word and Word Calm reviews ("It also gives
  you the definition which I like to know what the word means"); the Lexicon has
  them.
- **Dark mode** — mentioned in **0** of the 3,500 reviews, so it is not sold
  here (unlike in the Sudoku listing, where it was the most requested feature).

Below Words of Wonders and the Czech-made *Piknik Slovo*, the market tops out at
about 3,300 ratings, and every letter-wheel game that says it is made of Czech
words has 24 ratings or fewer. Wordscapes, the American leader, has 80 Czech
ratings. This is the storefront where a good listing actually moves the
ranking.

Three findings shaped `cs/`:

- **People search in Czech, for Czech.** Apple's suggestions: for *křížovk…* —
  `české křížovky`, `křížovkářský slovník`, `křížovky česky`, `křížovka`; for
  *slovní h…* — `české slovní hry` first; for *hry bez…* — `hry bez wifi`, `hry
  bez reklam`, `hry bez internetu`; for *hlavolam…* — `hlavolamy`, `hlavolamy
  česky`; for *hry na…* — `hry na mozek`. *české* and *česky* are in two of the top
  phrases, and Czech words are exactly what the competition lacks — which is why
  the first Czech screenshot says *Česká slova*.
- **The tops of the Czech result lists are tiny apps.** For "křížovky" the #1 is
  *iKřížovky* (5 ratings), for "česká slovní hra" *Mistr slova* (6) — only eight
  apps match that query at all.
- **Apple's Czech stemming cannot be trusted.** *hra* and *hry*, *křížovky* and
  *křížovka*, *české* and *česky* are not reliably linked, so the form people
  type — the one Apple suggests — is the one that goes in, and both forms of a
  word earn their bytes where both are suggested.

## What is in use

```
en-US   Wordwander: Word Game, No Ads   (29)   Calm offline crossword puzzles   (30)
cs      Wordwander: české křížovky      (26)   Slovní hra bez reklam, offline   (30)
```

Apple combines the terms of the name, the subtitle and the keyword field within
a locale, so between them `en-US` assembles *word game no ads*, *word games no
ads*, *word game offline*, *crossword offline*, *crossword no ads*, *crossword
puzzles*, *word puzzle no ads*, *calm word game* and *offline word games*
without spending a keyword character on any of them; with the keyword field,
*crossword no wifi*, *word games no internet*, *relaxing word game*, *word
games for seniors*, *word games for adults*, *daily word puzzle*, *word wheel*,
*word connect* and *word journey* join them.

`cs` assembles *české křížovky*, *slovní hra*, *křížovky bez reklam*, *hra bez
reklam*, *křížovky offline* and *slovní hra offline*, and with the keyword field
*české slovní hry*, *slovní hry*, *křížovky česky*, *hry bez reklam*, *hry bez
wifi*, *hry bez internetu*, *hlavolamy česky*, *logické hry* and *hry na mozek*.

Word order: `Word Game, No Ads`, not `No Ads Word Game` — the name reads left to
right, and the head term first reads as what the app is. In Czech, `české
křížovky` lower-case after the colon, as Czech writes it, and in the order Apple
suggests the phrase.

## The name

No app is called *Wordwander*, in the US or the Czech store, and Apple suggests
nothing for it. The nearest names:

| App | Developer | US / CZ ratings | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Word Wanderer** | 博 李 | 937 / 2 | "Connect letters to form words, unlock new levels" — the same genre, released December 2025, last updated March 2026 |
| Word Wander: Word Search | Jason David Browe | 7 / — | a word search, released August 2026 |
| Wanderword - Guess the words | XSGames | 0 / 0 | a guessing game |

*Word Wanderer* is the one to know about: the same kind of game under a name one
space and two letters away. App Store Connect should accept *Wordwander* as a
name — it is not the same string — and Apple suggests "word wanderer" when
someone types "word wander", so it is also a search that will show both apps. It
is not a reason to rename a game whose title screen, icon and display name are
already Wordwander, but it is worth knowing before launch — and if the game is
ever to be marketed outside the store, worth a trademark search.

Alternatives, if the name is refused or wanted different:

| English | | Czech | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordwander: Word Game, No Ads | (29) | Wordwander: české křížovky | (26) |
| Wordwander: Crossword, No Ads | (29) | Wordwander: slovní hra česky | (28) |
| Wordwander: Offline Crossword | (29) | Wordwander: křížovky bez reklam | (31 ✗) |
| Wordwander: Calm Word Puzzles | (29) | Wordwander: hra se slovy | (24) |
| Wordwander – Word Game | (22) | Wordwander: české slovní hry | (28) |

Recommendation: keep *no ads* in the English name — it is the modifier people
type most, it is what the competition fails at, and the long-tail table shows
that the name is what ranks for it. *Crossword, No Ads* is the closest second: it
trades the bigger *word game* for *crossword*, whose long tail ("crossword
offline", "crossword no ads") has the weakest competition of all. Worth an A/B
test of the product page once there is traffic — the name itself cannot be
tested, but the first screenshot's caption can.

Deliberately **not** the choice:

- Anything with *Wordscapes*, *Zen*, *Trip*, *Cookies*, *Wonders* or *Connect*
  leading — each is the start of a competitor's name, and a near-identical name
  buys their leftovers while making the listing look like a clone.
- *Free* / *zdarma* anywhere — guideline 2.3.7 (see README).
- *Relax* or *Zen* in the name — the "relax" names (Zen Word, Otium Word: Relax,
  Word Relax) are all ad-funded, and the word is in the subtitle's *calm* and the
  keyword field already.

## Keywords

In use:

```
en-US  relaxing,wifi,daily,connect,wheel,swipe,brain,senior,adult,anagram,spelling,timer,internet,journey  (98)
cs     hry,česky,křížovka,slova,slovo,wifi,internetu,hlavolamy,logické,mozek,relax,písmen,denní  (88 / 95 bytes)
```

Why these, in English:

- *wifi*, *internet* — with the name's *no*: *crossword no wifi* (Apple's #2 for
  "crossword n"), *word games no wifi*, *word games no internet*.
- *relaxing* — *relaxing word game*, *relaxing puzzle games*, *relaxing games*;
  the subtitle has *calm*, and Apple does not reliably stem one into the other.
- *daily* — *daily word puzzle*, *daily puzzles*, *daily word games*.
- *connect*, *wheel*, *swipe* — what the genre is called by people who don't
  know its brand names: *word connect* (Apple suggests ten variants of it),
  *word wheel* (#9 for "word w", ten suggestions of its own), *word swipe*.
  All three are generic words used in dozens of app names, not one competitor's.
- *brain* — *brain games* (and *brain games for seniors*), *word brain game*.
- *senior*, *adult* — *word games for seniors* and *word games for adults* are
  both top suggestions, and Apple pairs an English singular with its plural.
  (*kids* is left out on purpose: guideline 2.3.8 reserves "for kids" for the
  Kids category.)
- *anagram*, *spelling* — the two other words people use for the mechanic
  (*anagram games*, *spelling games for adults*).
- *timer* — with the name's *no*: *word game no timer*.
- *journey* — *word journey* is a suggestion whose top results have 93 to 603
  ratings, and the journey is the game's whole idea.

In Czech: *hry* (the plural the suggestions use: *české slovní hry*, *hry bez
reklam*), *česky* (*křížovky česky*, *hlavolamy česky*), *křížovka* (the singular,
suggested on its own), *slova* and *slovo* (*hledej slova*, *najdi slovo*,
*slovo křížek* are all suggestions), *wifi* and *internetu* (with the subtitle's
*bez*), *hlavolamy*, *logické* and *mozek* (*logické hry*, *hry na mozek*),
*relax* (*relax game* and *relaxační hry* are both suggestions, and Czech
stemming will not link the second to anything else), *písmen* (for searches
like *slova z písmen*, which describe the mechanic) and *denní* (*denní slovní
hra* is how the Czech-made daily word games describe themselves).

Rules, so that editing does not break it:

- Separate with a comma and **no space after the comma** — a space counts towards
  the limit of 100.
- Do not repeat words from the name or the subtitle, Apple indexes those
  separately — **between the name and the subtitle too.** That is why *word*,
  *game*, *no*, *ads*, *calm*, *offline*, *crossword* and *puzzles* are absent
  from the English list, and *české*, *křížovky*, *slovní*, *hra*, *bez*,
  *reklam* and *offline* from the Czech one.
- Do not put an English plural next to its singular, Apple pairs them itself.
  The Czech set does not get that service — see above.
- **Do not name competing games or apps** — grounds for rejection, and the
  temptation is strong in a genre this crowded. *wordscapes*, *zen* (Zen Word),
  *trip* (Word Trip), *cookies*, *wonders*, *crossy* and *nyt* are all other
  people's names. One combination is closer to that than it looks: the
  subtitle's *calm* and the name's *word* together match *Word Calm*, which is
  also an app's whole name (14,234 ratings). It stays, because *calm* describes
  the game and reads as a description in a sentence, not as a reference — the
  same reasoning as *master* in the Sudoku listing. If a reviewer ever raises
  it, *Relaxing offline crosswords* is the subtitle to swap in.
- Do not reach for a word just because it fits. *search* (word search is a
  different game), *wordle*, *kids*, *multiplayer*, *friends* and *trivia* are
  all popular and all lies here.
- No *free* / *zdarma*, here or anywhere else (guideline 2.3.7).
